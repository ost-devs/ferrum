# ferrum-state: design

The Rust design of `ferrum-state`, the port of `@codemirror/state` 6.7.6 (`docs/workflow/specification.md` §7). Written by the consultant; the record of each choice is in `docs/decisions/ferrum-state.md` ("S5, first part"; "The skeleton approved" for the forms the skeleton settled) and `docs/decisions/foundation.md`. What binds beside this file: `.claude/rules/decisions.md` (D1, D2, D5, D6, D11–D16), `.claude/rules/rust-naming-decisions.md` (N1–N17), and the decisions about single items in `docs/parity/ferrum-state/raw/decisions.jsonl`, which this file does not repeat. Where Ferrum can be better than cm6: `raw/improvements.jsonl` (section 9).

Every public signature is written in the skeleton, the first step of the implementation, from the rules here; the consultant approves the skeleton. What the fixture replayer does with each fixture is section 10.

## 2. Positions and numbers

- A position, a length, an offset, a line number, a count and an index are `usize` (D12). A signed distance is `isize` (`next(skip)`). Their unit is the unit of D1.
- A chunk of a range set holds the positions of its ranges relative to its start as signed distances, `isize`: cm6 stores a range added with `from > to`, and a range added after one, at a negative offset (`state.cm6.201.d1`, `state.RangeSetBuilder.add.B6`, `mirrors/cm6/state/src/rangeset.ts:531-532`). `__test::Chunk::from` and `to` give them as `&[isize]` (Q100).
- The unit is the UTF-8 byte (D1): a position is a byte offset into the document in which every line break counts one. A position must lie on a `char` boundary; one that does not is a violated precondition and panics (D13), as `str` slicing does. `Text::is_char_boundary(pos)` (`/// ferrum-only`) lets a caller test it. `code_point_at(s, pos) -> Option<char>`: `None` at or beyond the end, where cm6 gives `NaN`. `code_point_size(c: char) -> usize` is `c.len_utf8()`. `find_cluster_break`, `count_column`, `find_column` and `try_find_column` take and return byte offsets into the string they are given.
- A number cm6 uses as a sentinel outside the positions is `None`: `min_point_size: Option<usize>` of `RangeSet::compare` and `spans` (cm6's default -1, which differs from 0), the result of `try_find_column` (cm6's -1), of `try_map_pos` (cm6's `null`) (N13) and of `RangeCursor::rank` (cm6's -1 for a cursor over no layer, `mirrors/cm6/state/src/rangeset.ts:712`; every other rank cm6 gives, at the end too, is `Some`, Q97).
- Range sides (`start_side`, `end_side`) are `i32`; their difference is computed in `i64`. `1_000_000_000` is cm6's `C.Far` and is used wherever cm6 uses it (`state.cm6.187.d1`), except that in `RangeSet::eq` and `RangeSet::spans` an ended cursor's position compares greater than every bound a caller passes (`state.cm6.189.d2`, `state.cm6.190.d2`). A caller's query bound is any `usize` (D17).
- Outside `RangeSet`, a `1e9` that cm6 writes as "to the end" of a scan, and that no output shows, is no bound in Ferrum: the default `to` of `appendText` (`mirrors/cm6/state/src/text.ts:384`, `usize::MAX` in `crate::text::append_text`) and the position after the last pair in `ChangeSet.filter` (`mirrors/cm6/state/src/change.ts:273`, `state.cm6.032.d3`). Both give cm6's result for every text and change set of at most 1e9 positions; beyond it cm6 cuts a leaf's lines or does not terminate. `C.Far` stays because cursors report it.
- `goal_column` is `Option<f64>`; `bidi_level` `Option<u8>`; `assoc` `i8`.
- `Transaction::TIME` is `i64` milliseconds.

Other forms the skeleton fixed:
- `Text::iter_lines(range: Option<(usize, usize)>)`: `None` is cm6's call without `from` (the whole document, through `iter`); `Some((from, to))` its ranged form, `to` being `lines() + 1` where cm6's caller omits it (D14).

The function owns what it reads (cm6's closes over the `wordChars` string, `mirrors/cm6/state/src/state.ts:373`, `charCategorizer`), so a caller keeps it after the state is gone, as cm6's search does inside the test function of a query (`mirrors/cm6/search/src/search.ts:203`, `:287`). `use<>` says so in the type: in edition 2024 an `impl Trait` in return position captures the lifetime of `&self` unless told otherwise, and `'static` alone does not stop the borrow (rustc 1.97: E0716 without it, compiles with it).
- `Compartment::new()` (N15); `Prec` (N16); `FacetConfig.is_static` (N12); `RangeSet::iter_sets` (N14); `map_pos` and `try_map_pos`, `find_column` and `try_find_column` (N13).
- Field values, facet inputs and facet outputs are `PartialEq`, and `RangeSet<T>` is not. A field or a facet that holds a range set is `ferrum-view`'s case (its decoration sets): its specification decides between `PartialEq` by allocation for `RangeSet` and definitions that take a comparison in place of the bound. Either adds to this API and breaks no caller.
- Every module is public and every item is also exported at the crate root. Which paths are the stable ones is decided with the facade (`docs/foundation/ferrum-foundation.md` §4.1).

## 4. Core types and their invariants

- **`Text`** (D2). cm6's tree, ported as it is: a leaf holds at most 32 lines, a node at most 32 children built by cm6's `TextNode.from` and split by its `decompose`; `children()` exposes it. A line is stored as given, without its break; a break counts one position. `Text::of` copies its input and panics on an empty list. Lines are immutable UTF-8 strings behind `Arc`; `Line::text()` returns `&str`. Edits share untouched children. Splitting a string into lines is `EditorState`'s and `ChangeSet::of`'s job, on `\r\n?|\n` or on the `LINE_SEPARATOR` facet's exact string; joining uses `"\n"` or the separator given.
- **Grapheme clusters** (D2): the algorithm and the table of `@marijn/find-cluster-break` 1.0.4, ported, on `char`s. Every caller in the crate (`find_cluster_break`, `count_column`, `find_column`, `word_at`) reaches the rule through one private function, because `state.imp.001` replaces the rule in the crate's improvement delta and must change nothing else.
- **`ChangeDesc` / `ChangeSet`**: cm6's `sections` array of (length, inserted length or -1) pairs, kept exactly as cm6 builds it (`add_section`'s merging), since `Debug`, `to_json` and the walks expose it. `ChangeSet` adds the inserted `Text`s. Immutable, `Arc` inside.
- **`SelectionRange`**: `Copy`; `from`, `to`, the flags as cm6 packs them, `goal_column`. No invariant between `from` and `to` (`state.cm6.087.d1`). **`EditorSelection`**: at least one range; sorted and merged only by the paths that do so in cm6.
- **`RangeSet<T>`**: cm6's layers of chunks (`state.cm6.184.d1`), chunks behind `Arc` and shared exactly where cm6 shares them (`state.RangeSet.map.B2`, `state.RangeSet.update.B6`, `state.RangeSet^join.B5`). `RangeSetBuilder::finish(self)`.
- **`EditorState`**: an immutable handle (`Arc`), holding the configuration, the document, the selection and the slot values. Every dynamic slot is computed before the constructor returns. **`Transaction`**: immutable; caches its state.
- **`combine_config`**: `state.cm6.134.d1`.

## 5. Errors

One enum, `Error`, `#[non_exhaustive]`, a variant per explicit throw of `raw/throws.jsonl` that Ferrum can reach (25 of the 27 sites; sites that share a message share a variant; the two throws of `flatten` for a value that is no extension, `mirrors/cm6/state/src/facet.ts:550`, `:551`, are excluded by type and listed in `implementation.md`), carrying the numbers of the message; `Display` writes cm6's message. `from_json` readers return it; everywhere else the failure is `panic!("{}", Error::…)` (D13). The 47 implicit throws of `raw/throws.jsonl` are engine `TypeError`s: 41 need input that D12 excludes (among them `Text::iter` with a direction other than the two, `state.cm6.239.d1`; a field spec without `create` or `update`, `state.cm6.047.d1`); 1 is not reached: `makeCategorizer` reading a `wordChars` that is `null` or `undefined` (`mirrors/cm6/state/src/charcategory.ts:32`), since `char_categorizer` panics before it for any `wordChars` that is not a string, with the message of the table below (`state.cm6.029.d1`); 2 do not compile (`RangeSetBuilder` after `finish`, `mirrors/cm6/state/src/rangeset.ts:529`, `:561`); 3 panic with a message of Ferrum's own, because Ferrum's types can still express their input (`to_json` or `from_json` with a field that lacks the function, `mirrors/cm6/state/src/state.ts:213`, `:229`, `state.cm6.016.d1`; `replace_range` with an index beyond the ranges, `mirrors/cm6/state/src/selection.ts:192`). The skeleton step lists, for each of the 47, which of the three it is. Conformance found three more of the third kind, which the inventory had taken for unreachable (Q152): the reads of an insertion's text that cm6 does not guard (`ChangeSet.toJSON`, `mirrors/cm6/state/src/change.ts:305`; `iterChanges`, `:416`; `SectionIter.textBit`, `:563`), which meet a missing text in a set that `map` built from one `invert` was given a document shorter than the set (`state.ChangeSet.invert.B3`). They panic with one message of Ferrum's own, written by one private function that every such read goes through.

**Which functions document a panic.** Every public function through which a panic site can fire states, under `# Panics`, the message and the condition under which it fires through that function, or names the public function whose text states it (`where [`new_doc`](Transaction::new_doc) panics`). That holds for cm6's messages and for these alike, and for a site reached through another public function (`Transaction::reconfigured` through `Transaction::state`; `EditorState::field` and `EditorState::facet` read by a field's own function during construction). A function through which a site is reached by the code but whose own inputs cannot meet its condition says nothing of it. Its inputs are every value its types allow, a state built by the public API among them. So a function that reaches a site only through public functions whose inputs cannot meet the condition says nothing either, since what it passes them are values of their types. A site that no input reaches (its panic line `UNREACHABLE`) is stated by no function. A panic that a caller's own function raises by calling a public function belongs to that call, whose text states it. It does not belong to the function that ran the caller's function: `EditorState::create` does not state `field`'s panic when a field's `create` calls `field` for an absent field. A function that relies on a public callee's text links it. Text without a link does not count, because a caller reads the function's own entry and nothing else. The static list of the pairs is `conf_check panics --reach`, on the call graph the compiler resolves (plan "Conformance" step 5 (a)). D13's purpose is that a caller can test first what the text states (2026-11-27; Q151).

The replayer never compares these messages: the calls are left out (`char`, a row's id), or the recorded error is a `TypeError`, and then the op leaves the message out (section 10, "Engine errors").

How a test checks a panic: `std::panic::catch_unwind` (around `AssertUnwindSafe`: the crate's values hold `dyn` contents), comparing the payload's text with the fixture's `message` (numbers that are positions mapped to the unit of D1). The error's `name` in a fixture is not compared, with one use: an error named `TypeError` is an engine error (all 47 implicit throws and no explicit one), so Ferrum must panic there and the message, which is Ferrum's own, is not compared.

## 6. Ownership and threading (D6)

Every public value type is immutable, cheap to clone and `Send + Sync`; callbacks the crate keeps are `Fn + Send + Sync + 'static` and user values `Send + Sync + 'static`; a callback called only before the call returns has none of these bounds (D14). Five public types only carry such a callback into one call and are therefore neither `Send` nor `Sync` (D6): `FacetConfig` (its field `enables`) and `FacetEnables` (its variant `Function`), which `Facet::define` consumes; `StateFieldSpec` (its field `provide`), which `StateField::define` consumes; `RangeSetUpdate` (its field `filter`), which `RangeSet::update` consumes; `StateCommandTarget` (its field `dispatch`), which a `StateCommand` receives for one call. Nothing the crate returns or holds contains one of them. G1's check (`plan.md` "Conformance") accepts these five, each for the member named here, and no other type. The crate starts no thread and holds no global mutable state but the id counter. A state under construction is visible only to the callbacks that build it, on the thread that builds it. Such a callback may read it, which computes its slots on demand as cm6 does (`state.ts:58`), and may keep a clone of it; once the construction has finished, the clone is that state. A clone used outside its build gets nothing cm6 does not give: a transaction that starts from it panics (`state.cm6.100.d2`), whether its build is still running or panicked; and reading a slot of it not yet computed, on a thread other than the one that builds it, panics with Ferrum's own message `A state under construction is read from a thread that is not building it` (section 5), so that no two threads compute one slot and no user function of a slot runs twice (Q142, `docs/decisions/ferrum-state.md`, "Q141 and Q142"). After a build that panicked, reads on its own thread go on computing on demand, as in cm6. Determinism in tests: ids follow definition order, so a test defines its facets and fields on one thread.

## 7. `StateCommand` (D5)

`pub struct StateCommandTarget<'a> { pub state: EditorState, pub dispatch: &'a mut dyn FnMut(Transaction) }` and `pub type StateCommand = dyn for<'a> Fn(StateCommandTarget<'a>) -> bool + Send + Sync`. A function item and a closure are both commands; a test builds the target as cm6's tests build their `{state, dispatch}` literal.

## 9. Native opportunities and improvements

No mechanism is replaced for a native one: the stack notes of `raw/behaviors.jsonl` name mechanisms that exist for JavaScript (identity shortcuts, sentinels, the fixnum bound), and each is either kept because fixtures and the oracle observe it, or replaced as section 3 says.

Where Ferrum can be better than cm6 is `raw/improvements.jsonl` (`.claude/rules/decisions.md` D16), ten rows on 2026-11-13. None is part of this design: none would break the public API if made later, so each is made, or settled, after the floor is shown (`plan.md` "Improvement delta"). The position unit (D1) and the sharing rules (D6) are the two choices that had to be made here, because changing them later breaks every caller. `state.imp.001`, the Unicode cluster rule, is scheduled; the others are candidates with the point at which each is evaluated.

## 10. The fixture replayer

What the replayer of `crates/ferrum-state/tests/` does with the fixtures of the five chunks and the ops of `raw/fixture-ops.jsonl` (record: `docs/decisions/ferrum-state.md`, "S5, second part"). The "Implementation" section of `plan.md` says who writes it and when; this section says what it must do, and it may do nothing else of its own accord: a case these rules do not settle is a question.

**One evaluator, in cm6's unit.** The replayer implements the language of `tools/cm6/program.ts` once and each chunk's ops from the header of its `interp.ts`, against Ferrum. A program runs as written, in UTF-16 code units: the harness ops (arithmetic, `ints`, `count`, `slice`, comparisons) compute on the numbers the input holds. Conversion to the unit of D1 happens where a number passes between the program and Ferrum and nowhere else: a position, offset or length given to Ferrum is converted to bytes through the text it is measured on, and one read from Ferrum is converted back, so that outputs are compared in the unit they were recorded in. A position in a document whose text the input does not hold (a `ChangeSet` built from a length alone, a `RangeSet`) is converted as in a document of ASCII text, and the length of inserted text through that text. Which numbers are positions and which text each is measured on: `raw/positions.jsonl`, read with the rules of `plan.md` "Implementation" ("Positions").

**Values.** A JavaScript value of a program is one of:
- a primitive (number, string, boolean, `null`, `undefined`), compared by value;
- an object or array the program itself builds (a data literal, `let`, `list`, `record`, `object`, `fill`, `copy`, a getter's `fresh` or `const` value, a function built by `fn` or by a callback spec): a reference with identity, which the harness ops may mutate. Given to Ferrum as a user value (a facet input, a field value, an effect or annotation value, a function-valued input) it is a type whose `PartialEq` is value equality for primitives and pointer equality for objects, arrays and functions: what cm6's `===` is, and what D14 prescribes for a type cm6 compares by identity. Given to Ferrum as typed data (the lines of `Text::of`, a JSON value, a spec, a configuration struct, a `PHRASES` map) it is converted, and Ferrum's value has no link to it;
- a `RangeValue` of chunk `rangeset`: one Rust type for the classes `V`, `W` and `Id`, which carries the index of its value spec outside its equality. A value's index in a result is that index. Its `PartialEq`: the same index is equal and logs nothing (`mirrors/cm6/state/src/rangeset.ts:39`, `a == b`); different classes are unequal and log nothing (the same line, `a.constructor == b.constructor`); otherwise the pair is logged and the class decides (`V`: equal names; `W`: equal; `Id`: unequal);
- a value of Ferrum.

**Key order.** The replayer's own objects keep their keys as JavaScript does (integer-like keys ascending, then the others in the order they were set), and a fixture file is read with its keys in file order, so a program computes and prints what `tools/cm6/program.ts` does. A JSON object Ferrum returns has no member order (section 3): it enters the program as an object marked unordered, with its members in the order `serde_json` gives. An output position that holds an unordered object is compared member by member; every other object is compared with its key order. An op that reads the order of the keys of an unordered object (`Object.keys`, `Object.entries`, `for … in` in its `interp.ts` code) is a question, unless `raw/replay.jsonl` already gives that part of its result no counterpart. `JSON.stringify` of an object Ferrum returned reads that order: the string is a part with no counterpart, which the op's row names (`stringified` of `range.toJSON` and `sel.toJSON`; Q89); a harness op that stringifies such an object and parses it back (`jsonCopy`) gives an object marked unordered. A `fields` argument is given to Ferrum as the slice of its entries in the program object's key order, which is the order cm6 walks it in.

**Identity.** `same {a, b}` (and `allSame`, `sameItems`, a `same` field of `scenario`, an op of `raw/replay.jsonl` whose note names this table):
- both primitives: value equality; one a primitive and the other not: false (a value of Ferrum stands for an object of cm6, and JavaScript's `===` of an object and a primitive is false);
- both references the program built: pointer equality;
- one a list or JSON value Ferrum returned (the array or object that cm6's call gave back, and every array and object inside it), the other not a primitive: no counterpart, `identity`, even against itself. An op marks such a value when it returns it, through the means the core gives; an array or object that the op's code in `interp.ts` builds from what cm6 returned (a list it collects, a record of fields it reads) is a reference the program built. `copy`, `slice` and every other harness op that builds a new array or object give an unmarked one;
- both of one Ferrum type of this table: the identity the table gives;
- anything else (a value of Ferrum against a reference the program built, two Ferrum values of different types or of a type outside the table): no counterpart, with the reason `identity` (below). `countOf` follows the same rules for each member it compares.

| Ferrum value | Identity | Read through |
|---|---|---|
| `Facet`, `FacetReader` of it, `StateField`, `Compartment`, `StateEffectType`, `AnnotationType` | the definition (section 3) | `PartialEq` |
| `Extension` | what it was converted from (section 3) | `__test` |
| a value read from a state (`facet`, `field`) | of the user value, when the read gives one (a field value, a combined output that is a program reference); else the address of the slot's value (the default output list) | `std::ptr::eq` on the references read |
| `Text` | the node (`Text::empty()` is one node; `Text::of([""])` gives it, `mirrors/cm6/state/src/text.ts:155`) | `__test` |
| `RangeSet<T>`, a chunk | the shared allocation (the empty set is one) | `__test` |
| `EditorState`, its configuration | the shared allocation | `__test` |

`SelectionRange`, `EditorSelection`, `ChangeDesc`, `ChangeSet`, `Transaction`, `StateEffect`, `Annotation`, `Line`, `Range`, a cursor, and every list or JSON value Ferrum returns have no identity: they are values, and nothing a Rust caller can write tells one copy from another. The types of the table are those where identity is a behavior a caller or a performance target depends on: a definition is a key; an unchanged slot, document, range set or state is how the code above skips work; shared `Text` nodes and chunks are what keeps an edit from costing the size of the document (section 4). `ferrum-view`'s specification may show a consumer of another identity (`mirrors/cm6/lint/src/lint.ts:373` compares two documents); the function then becomes public and its type joins the table.

**No counterpart.** A call evaluates to the value *no counterpart*, and Ferrum is not called, when:
1. an argument cannot be written in the signature of the skeleton (D12): the reason `type`, with the argument and its value. A value that cm6's code at that argument treats exactly as the absent value, or reads only for truthiness, is not such an argument: it is given to Ferrum as D12 says (the absent value `None`, or the `bool` of the test, after the parameter's default for `undefined`), and the op cites cm6's line (D12; Q91, Q109);
2. a `(pos)` argument falls inside a character of its text (D1): `char`;
3. the call meets the condition a row of `raw/decisions.jsonl` states ("Condition for the replayer"): the row's id;
4. `raw/replay.jsonl` gives the op `none`, or the part of its result `part` names: `op`;
5. an argument is itself no counterpart;
6. an identity comparison is outside the rules of "Identity": `identity`. Ferrum was called for both operands; only the comparison is left out.

In cases 1 to 5 the call *was not made*. Three things follow from a call that was not made:
- *Its arguments are evaluated all the same*, in the order the op's code in `interp.ts` evaluates them, an op given `none` included: an argument that throws, meets a row's condition or advances a cursor does so as on cm6, and the trace keeps cm6's lines (`plan.md` "The replayer").
- *What it would have changed is unknown.* A value of Ferrum that the call would have changed (a builder it adds to, a cursor or iterator it advances: the receivers Ferrum takes as `&mut`) is no counterpart from then on, in every later use (case 5). So is a value of the program that a callback given to the call writes when it is called: the counter a callback spec names (`{set: …, counter: "n"}`, F7109), and the variables a call of a `fn` of the program writes: its `params`, every name its body binds with `as`, and every variable its body's `array.push` grows (the variable its `of` names). Which `fn` the call would reach through the configuration is not read: the variables of every `fn` evaluated before the call are no counterpart from then on, whether or not they held a value (Q119 entry, F7250). The same holds for a call during which a callback returned a value the signature cannot hold ("A value a callback returns").
- *Whether cm6 threw there is unknown.* A `try` or a `capture` inside which a call was not made evaluates to no counterpart, whatever its steps gave.

An output position that holds no counterpart is not compared; every other position is compared exactly. One member may be absent from the recorded object: where cm6's op writes a member only for one class of the result and Ferrum's type cannot be that class (`json` of `desc.composeDesc`, `desc.mapDesc`, `set.mapDesc`, written by cm6 only when the result is a `ChangeSet`; section 3, Q87), the op writes it as no counterpart marked *may be absent*, and a member so marked that the recorded object lacks is left out of the comparison of members and of key order. Nothing else is marked so. A fixture is `equal` only when every call was made and every position compared; one compared but for what was left out is `part`. A fixture is *not compared* as a whole (`none`):
- when no position of its output is compared: its whole output is no counterpart, or every leaf of the arrays and objects it holds is (an empty array or object is a compared position). The shape is compared all the same: an array of another length or an object with other keys is a mismatch. What is equal there is the length of a list or the names of fields, which in most such fixtures the program wrote and Ferrum did not, so the fixture counts for no behavior and the control run does not alter it;
- when the program's course depends on the value: the condition of an `if`, a member of `all` (which stops at the first falsy member), the list of a `map`. The program stops there; the report adds an entry with the reason `flow` and that op;
- when its recorded output is an uncaught error, a call was not made, and Ferrum's run does not end in that error: the call not made may be the one that threw. The report adds an entry with the reason `error` and no op (`"op": null`): which call not made cm6 threw in is not in the fixture, and no rule of the chain's shape gives it (Q120); `handoff` finds it in cm6's trace, checks that Ferrum did not make it, and prints the origins grouped. When the run does end in the recorded error, the fixture is `part`. When the recorded output is a value, a call not made excuses nothing: an error of Ferrum, or another value, is a mismatch;
- when it runs an op `raw/replay.jsonl` gives `skip`: the program stops when the op is reached, with the reason `op`;
- when a row of `raw/decisions.jsonl` that states no condition lists it: the reason is the row's id;
- when its recorded output is a timeout (`{"timeout": ms}`: cm6 did not return). Such a fixture is never run, since the replayer has no time limit, and a row that states no condition must list it; one that no row lists fails the run.

**Engine errors.** Where cm6 threw an engine `TypeError` and Ferrum panics with its own message of section 5 (the sites listed there), the op throws an error named `TypeError` whose message is no counterpart, with the reason `engine` and the op: the name is compared, the message is not. The op does so only for a panic whose message is that site's message of section 5 exactly; any other panic is an error named as `thrown` names it, with Ferrum's message, and is compared. A `capture` that catches it holds the message as no counterpart. In the file of what is left out, an `engine` entry is read against the table of section 5 (Q88).

**What a row of `raw/decisions.jsonl` lists.** The `fixtures` of a row are the fixtures the row leaves out, whatever its class: the calls that meet its condition when it states one, the whole fixture when it states none. A row lists a fixture only where the replayer cannot find what is left out by cases 1, 2 and 4: an input excluded by type is found from the signature and carries the reason `type`, and is listed by no row. So a row without a condition is a deviation whose result Ferrum gives differently for the whole fixture, or a recorded timeout. Both kinds of list are checked by the run:
- the replayer applies a row's condition only in the fixtures the row lists, and fails when a listed fixture does not meet it: the lists were computed by running every fixture on cm6 with the row's deviation written into it (`docs/decisions/ferrum-state.md`, "S5, second part"), so the two must agree;
- a fixture listed by a row without a condition is run like any other (a recorded timeout excepted), and the replayer fails when its output equals the recorded one: the row then leaves out a fixture Ferrum reproduces. Any other end of the run is `none`.

"End of the run" means that the program ran: it gave an output, or stopped for a reason of this section. A listed fixture is like any other in what is not such an end: one that reaches a `todo!()` is `pending` (code that does not exist says nothing about the row, and `pending` is zero at the hand-off), and a failure of the replayer itself (a value it has no form for, a panic outside an op's call) is a `mismatch` that names the replayer. A row's condition is judged only in a program that ran to its output: there, a listed fixture in which no call met the condition is a `mismatch`; a program that stopped earlier (`flow`, `op`, `pending`) is not judged.

**A value a callback returns.** A value cm6 reads only for truthiness (what a `compare` returns, `mirrors/cm6/state/src/facet.ts:166`, `:180`, `:198`, `:238`, `:250`, `:333`, `:498`) is returned to Ferrum as the `bool` of that test (D12; Q109). A callback the program supplies may otherwise return what the signature cannot hold (an odd-length array from a change filter, a key beyond `effects` and `annotations` from an extender). The call of Ferrum during which the callback returned it is then no counterpart, case 1, with the value; what Ferrum computed from a stand-in is discarded.

**`raw/replay.jsonl`**, one row per chunk and op whose kinds are not only `api` or only `harness`: `run` (against the public API, compared whole), `test` (needs a member of `__test`, D15), `part` (`parts` names what of its result is no counterpart), `identity` (the rules above), `none` (the result is no counterpart; the op is written all the same, and evaluates its arguments), `skip` (the op changes an object after it was handed over or needs a second copy of the package: a fixture that runs it is not compared). An op whose kinds are only `api` has a row when it is not `run` (`value.set` of chunk `rangeset`, `skip`; `mapmode.values` of chunk `change`, `none`, Q86; `range.toJSON` and `sel.toJSON` of chunk `change`, `part`, Q89). An op that observes callbacks is `run`: the replayer's callbacks record what the header says, and the order and number of calls are compared (D14).

**What is left out is a file, not a habit.** The replayer writes, for every fixture that is not compared whole, `{chunk, id, compared: "part" | "none", reasons: [{reason, op, argument or part, value}]}` to `docs/parity/ferrum-state/fixtures/no-counterpart.jsonl`. The file is written once, when the replayer first runs on all five chunks, read and approved by the consultant by reason (the `type` and `char` entries against D12 and D1, `op` and a row's id against their rows, `identity` against the table of "Identity" and `state.cm6.229.d1`, `flow` by the entry that precedes it, `error` by cm6's trace of the fixture, in which the evaluation that threw must be one Ferrum did not make, `engine` against the table of section 5), and committed; from then on the replayer fails on any difference between what it leaves out and the file, and reports per behavior how many of its fixtures were compared whole, in part and not at all. A behavior none of whose fixtures is compared needs a written test or a decision, and is listed.

**Time.** `now` is replayed as `Date.now()` is: whole milliseconds since the Unix epoch, truncated, read from `std::time::SystemTime` when the op is evaluated. A `Transaction.time` Ferrum read from the clock (`state.cm6.105.d1`) is the value Ferrum gives, like any other, and an explicit one is compared whatever its size (F0511: 1e15). So what a program computes from the clock (F0448: an integer, between two reads of `now` around the update) is compared exactly, and no time is left out. No recorded output holds the clock's number itself: `json.ann` writes a `Transaction.time` above 1e12 as the unstable `wall-clock` (`jAnn` in `docs/parity/ferrum-state/oracle/cm6/state/interp.ts`), and the replayer's `json.ann` does the same; a value `js_unstable` in an output is compared as present (Q113).
