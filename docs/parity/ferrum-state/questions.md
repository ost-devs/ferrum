## Q149. The rule of `state.cm6.126.d1`, written into cm6 as its row words it, changes effect identity, which the fixtures record and Ferrum does not
- **Found.** The row says its patch "writes the rule into cm6 (a mapped value always makes a new effect)". Run as a probe and not committed, a patch that keeps the effect where `Object.is(mapped, this.value)` and makes a new one otherwise changes no committed fixture. No committed fixture has a `map` that returns a zero of the other sign. The patch as the row words it is in `.scratch/patches-pending/state.cm6.126.d1.patch`, not committed: a patch that fails `check` is not used (`tools/oracle_patch.py` docstring), and G5 at step 5 (f) runs every committed patch. - **Open because.** The row states the patch's rule in words that cover two things: the value held (`-0.0` against `0.0`, the deviation) and the effect's identity, which `design.md` §10 says Ferrum does not have. Plan step 5 (a) asks for the patch "run by `oracle_patch.py check` over the committed fixtures, the changed fixtures its row's list". Nothing in force says whether a fixture whose only change under a patch is in records the replayer leaves out belongs on the row's list.
- **Bears on it.** The 10 fixtures are already left out where they differ (by `identity`), so the replay's verdicts on them do not depend on the list. A row's list is what G5 reads as the fixtures the deviation covers. Plan step 5 (c) adds fixtures of `state.cm6.126.d2` and step 5 (d) a written test of `state.cm6.126.d1` (`-0.0` kept), since no fixture has the signed-zero case.
- **Blocks.** The (a) item for `state.cm6.126.d1`: the committed patch and the row's list. Nothing else of (a); (b) does not wait on it.
