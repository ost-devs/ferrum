# ferrum-state: verifier card (conformance, steps 3 and 4)

Written by the orchestrator from `docs/parity/ferrum-state/plan.md` "Conformance", step 3, and `docs/workflow/conformance.md` Appendix A. Your assignment (the spawn prompt) names your unit, your run (*tests*, *hazards* or *gaps*, or two of them; `plan.md` step 3, M27) and your budget. The crate is at the recorded commit c08e6da (the tree's `crates/ferrum-state/` holds it; nothing under `src/` changed since). Reference: `@codemirror/state` 6.7.6 in `mirrors/cm6/state/`.

## What your verdicts are for

The conformance verdict of the crate says whether it does what cm6 does, as the approved specification records it, and nothing else but what a decision allows. The tools have already run what data can settle (every fixture replayed with 0 mismatch, the control runs, 25,638 fresh cases, coverage, mutation; their output is in `docs/parity/ferrum-state/conformance/`, whose README lists each file). What is left is what only a reading shows: whether a test asserts what its behavior says, whether a ported case is cm6's case, whether a hazard has a check, whether a surviving mutant or a missed branch is a gap. A verdict that passes something wrong is the one error nothing after you catches; a finding you are unsure of is still written, with what you checked.

You did not write the tests or the code, and you fix nothing: you write verdicts. You decide nothing either: where the specification and the decisions in force do not settle a point, the verdict is `UNVERIFIED` with the question, in the shape of `CLAUDE.md` "Raising a question", in your report.

## What you check, for each item of your unit in the sample

A *tests* run does 1 and 2; a *hazards* run does 3, 6 and 7 for the item groups its assignment names, the U4b replayer read ("Per unit"), writes one line of kind `hazard` per hazard, and writes the `item` line of each sampled item of those groups that has none yet, after reading the lines your unit's file holds for it; a *gaps* run does 4 and 5, and the G5 mismatches its assignment names. A run of several kinds does all of theirs.

1. **G2, written tests.** Every test of `written_<unit>.rs` whose `// covers:` names a behavior of your unit (every one, sampled or not): it asserts that behavior, on the input class the behavior states, on the whole observable result the behavior states. Otherwise `WEAKER`, with what it leaves unasserted.
2. **G3, ported cases.** The cm6 cases of the sampled items: the Rust test has cm6's scenario and assertions (`EQUIVALENT`), or differs only where a `// literal` comment gives a decided cause (`CHANGED`); otherwise `WEAKER`, `DIFFERENT` or `MISSING`. An argument cm6 omits and the Rust call writes with cm6's default (D14) is no difference and leaves the test `EQUIVALENT`; its `// literal` comment, which names D14 and says the caller writes the default, stays, since it is the cause G3's literal check reads (Q146); `conf_check tests` derives the label from the comments and fails where yours differs.
3. **G7, hazards.** Each hazard of `conformance/hazards-<chunk>.md` on a behavior of your unit, traced one by one: it has a check (a fixture whose `behaviors` names it and that is compared, a ported case or a written test) that exercises it: identity kept where cm6 relies on it, cached computations computed once, re-entrancy as in cm6, orders kept, ids deterministic (D6). A hazard no check exercises is a finding. Two kinds of hazard need no check, and each has its own verdict, because the reader of the verdict checks a different thing for each (Q137):
   - `EXCLUDED`: a decision gives the case another result than cm6's, so cm6's behavior on it is not what Ferrum must show: either no input a caller can write in Ferrum reaches it (a type excludes the input: D12, D1, a fieldless enum; the case needs object identity or mutation after hand-over: D11 b, `design.md` §10; it is the JavaScript error class: D13; a constructor refuses the state it needs: a row of `raw/decisions.jsonl`), or the caller can still write the input and the decision says what Ferrum gives instead (its own `Vec` changed after `Text::of`, which `state.cm6.229.d1` copies; a byte position inside a character, which D1 makes a panic). The evidence names the decision (a `D` id, a row id or a `design.md` section) and the Ferrum form that excludes the case (the type, the signature, the field's visibility, the copy, or the constructor with the check that shows it refuses), and, where the caller can still write the input, the check that asserts the decided result; with no such check the line is a `FINDING`. The reader checks that decision. `EXERCISED` is kept for a check that compares cm6's behavior on the hazard's input, converted by D1, never for one that asserts a decided result in its place: an `EXERCISED` line tells its reader that cm6's behavior there is proven (Q145).
   - `UNOBSERVABLE`: no caller of cm6 can observe it either (a JavaScript string is immutable, so its aliasing shows nothing). The evidence cites cm6's code that shows it. TypeScript's `readonly` and `private` are not such evidence: they are erased at run time, so a JavaScript caller can write the field (then the case is `EXCLUDED` by D11 b, if Ferrum's field is not public).
   A hazard of which only a part is excluded or unobservable gets the verdict of the part a caller can write (`EXERCISED` or `FINDING`), with the excluded part and its decision in the evidence. `UNREACHABLE` is a verdict of branch outcomes and lines, never of a hazard.
4. **Surviving mutants** of your unit (`mutants-missed.md`, section `missed`; a `hang` is caught and not yours; a `timeout` still listed is not judged yet): judge each by running it. Write a probe, a cargo package under `.scratch/verify-<unit>/probe` that depends on `ferrum-state` by path with the feature `__test` and prints one line per input with what the mutant could change, then run `python3 tools/conf_check.py ferrum-state mutant-probe <unit> --probe .scratch/verify-<unit>/probe .scratch/conformance/ferrum-state/mutation .scratch/conformance/ferrum-state/mutation-2` (`--timeout <s>` per run, default 900): it applies each mutant to a copy of the crate and prints `equal` or the first line that differs. Which inputs reach a mutant is your judgment. Each mutant is either a behavior no check forbids (a finding: the behavior, and the input that would show the difference) or a mutant equivalent to the code (the reason it changes nothing observable).
5. **Coverage**: each missed branch outcome and each unsorted line of your unit in `coverage.txt` (`g5/coverage-fresh.txt` names a new case of G5 that reaches some of them): an input no check runs (a finding, with the input that reaches it) or an outcome no input reaches (with what excludes it).
6. **G9**: each `unwrap`, `expect` and `panic!` of your unit in `lists.md`: reachable only by a violated precondition the doc comment states (`# Panics`), or a finding. The `# Panics` of every public function through which the site is reached states every condition under which it fires there: a message quoted for one case and raised in another (`state.cm6.156.d2`, equal lengths) is a finding. `raw/throws.jsonl` gives the condition of the cm6 throw a site ports; `conf_check panics` checks the message and the function, never the condition (Q148).
7. **G7 lists**: for each piece of shared state of your unit in `lists.md`, whether two threads reaching it first at once can change what a caller observes (an id's order, how many times a user callback runs, an identity): a finding with the test that would show it where they can. For each sentinel, whether the arithmetic on it can overflow or wrap on a value the types allow: a finding with the input where it can. D17 says which values those are. A bound a caller passes that cm6 only compares (a query's end, a filter's range, a cursor's start, a size threshold) takes every `usize`, `usize::MAX` included, and must give cm6's result: a cast that wraps it is a `FINDING`. A position cm6 checks against the document must panic as cm6 throws for every `usize`: a cast that skips the check is a `FINDING`. A length or position Ferrum holds is at most `isize::MAX` by D17, so arithmetic on held values inside that bound is `SAFE` with D17 as its reason; the place that builds such a value from values in memory, or reads it from outside data, is where D17 is checked, and its missing check is the one `FINDING` (`state.cm6.160.d1` for the JSON readers). A stored `RangeSet` position at or above 1e9 is outside the domain of `state.cm6.187.d1`, and arithmetic reached only through one is `SAFE` with that row as its reason. Recursion whose depth grows with the document or with data made from it is a `FINDING` (D11 a).

## What you write

One line per verdict to `docs/parity/ferrum-state/raw/conformance-<unit>.jsonl` (`<unit>` as your assignment names it), appended as you go so that a stop loses nothing (a line is replaced only where your assignment names it):
`{"id", "kind": "item" | "behavior" | "hazard" | "test" | "mutant" | "branch" | "line" | "panic" | "shared" | "sentinel", "verdict", "evidence", "repro"}`. `id` is the row, test, mutant name or `src/<file>:<line>` it is about, and for a hazard `<behavior id>:h<n>` as `hazards` prints it; `verdict` one of `docs/workflow/conformance.md` §0 (items: `CONFORMANT`, `DECIDED DEVIATION`, `NONCONFORMANT`, `UNVERIFIED`; tests: `EQUIVALENT`, `CHANGED`, `WEAKER`, `DIFFERENT`, `MISSING`); for a hazard `EXERCISED` (with the check that exercises it), `EXCLUDED`, `UNOBSERVABLE` or `FINDING` (item 3); for the lists, mutants and coverage `FINDING`, `EQUIVALENT MUTANT`, `UNREACHABLE`, `PRECONDITION`, `SAFE`; `evidence` cites `file:line` on both sides; `repro` is the input that shows a finding (empty otherwise). A `FINDING` in a function that carries no `cm6:` line (a private helper) also has `"items": [<inventory id>, …]`, the items whose public calls reach it, where `conf_check items` cannot tie it by the call rule it prints: the item verdicts are derived from the lines, and a finding tied to no item would let its items pass. The other verifiers write their own files; the sixth verifier re-runs a sample of your reproductions.

**Check, run by you before you report:** every line of your file parses as JSON with those five keys (and `items` on a finding where the paragraph above asks it), and, after a hazards run, every hazard of its groups has a line of kind `hazard` and every sampled item of its groups a line of kind `item` (the value of `unit` there is, for example, `U1 text` or `U4 state (facet)`) (`python3 -c` over the two files is enough; print both counts).

**Budget.** Your assignment gives it, estimated by `docs/workflow/README.md` §5 "Budgets" for your run. What fills a *tests* run is reading; a *hazards* run is filled by reading and as much by the lines it writes (about 330 tokens a line with the reasoning behind it, M29): read the part of a file you need, keep what you extract in your verdict lines rather than reading it again, and give a line the evidence its reader needs, not more. A *gaps* run is filled as much by its own reasoning about each mutant and outcome (M28): settle one, append its line, and do not reread the code you have already judged. Your size is watched from your transcript, and you are stopped at one and a half times the budget; what you have appended by then is kept.

**Report:** the counts of your verdicts by kind and verdict; every finding and every `UNVERIFIED` with a one-line reason; the ids you could not check and why; the questions; then the two lines of `CLAUDE.md` "Reporting from a card". Do not commit: the orchestrator merges your file.

## Step 4: the re-check of step 3's verdicts

Written by the consultant from `plan.md` "Conformance" step 4 (M30, M32), for the `rechecker` agent. Your assignment names your part: a file of `.scratch/conformance/ferrum-state/recheck-parts/`, each line a drawn verdict line with what step 3's verifier was given for it (`sources`), never its verdict. The "Sources" above and the decisions in force are yours too; your first read is the same `lib.rs`. Step 3's verdict files are not: a hook keeps them, the re-check rows and the documents that summarize outcomes out of your context, and the tools below are the only way to a recorded line.

**What it is for.** A step-3 verdict that passes something wrong (a hazard called exercised by a check that does not reach it, an equivalent mutant that is not, a test called equivalent that asserts less) is the one error nothing later shows: the consultant reads every finding, and nobody reads the passes again. Your rows measure how often such a verdict is wrong, by kind and by run, and any wrong one makes the consultant correct its whole kind. An agreement counts only when your verdict was written before the recorded one reached you.

**For each line of your part, in this order.**
1. From its `sources`, the code at c08e6da, cm6 and the decisions in force, derive the verdict as "What you check" items 3 to 7 or 1 and 2 define it for its kind (for a test, `CHANGED` is any difference of scenario, argument or expected value that a `// literal` comment gives a decided cause for). Write it: `python3 tools/conf_check.py ferrum-state recheck-derive <<'EOF'` then a JSON row or list, `{"file", "line", "derived", "evidence", "repro"}` (`repro` may be empty), then `EOF`, as a command of its own. `evidence` is your own reason, with `file:line` citations. Lines on one function may be derived together and written in one call.
2. Then see the recorded lines: `python3 tools/conf_check.py ferrum-state recheck-reveal <file>:<line> ...`. It refuses a line whose derivation is not written, fills `recorded`, and freezes `derived` and `evidence`.
3. Where they differ, or the recorded reason differs from yours, re-run what settles it: the test (`cargo test -p ferrum-state --features __test <name>`), a probe through `mutant-probe` for a mutant, a probe on cm6 in the harness or one against Ferrum for a hazard, an input for an outcome. What each kind's check is: `plan.md` step 4, "What a re-check does". A `SAFE` written before D17 was judged against every value of the type; judge it under D17, and where only D17 changes the answer the outcome is `later decision`. Then `python3 tools/conf_check.py ferrum-state recheck-settle <<'EOF'` with `{"file", "line", "outcome", "severity", "settled", "repro"}`: `outcome` `agree`; `agree, evidence wrong` (the verdict stands on another reason than the one recorded: say which in `settled`); `overturn`; `later decision`. `severity` (`high` when the wrong verdict hides a difference a caller observes, `low` otherwise) only on an overturn. `settled` says what you re-ran and what it showed, and is needed on every row but an `agree` whose derivation equals the recorded verdict. If the re-run shows your own derivation wrong and the recorded verdict right, the outcome is `agree` and the derivation stays as written.

A plain agreement may be settled with `{"file", "line", "outcome": "agree"}` for several lines in one call. The rows go to `docs/parity/ferrum-state/raw/recheck.jsonl` through these tools only; you change no line of `raw/conformance-*.jsonl`.

**Check before you report:** `python3 tools/conf_check.py ferrum-state recheck --seed 20261126 --results docs/parity/ferrum-state/raw/recheck.jsonl --unit <your unit> --lines <your part>` (every line of your part has one complete row, its derivation the one the reveal froze).

**Budget.** Your assignment gives it (`docs/workflow/README.md` §5 "Budgets"): a fixed part and a figure per line. Derive, reveal and settle one line or one function's lines at a time, so that a run cut by a limit leaves complete rows; do not reread code you have judged.

**Report:** counts by kind and outcome; every row that is not a plain agreement, one line each; the ids you could not check and why; anything the hook blocked that you needed; then the two lines of `CLAUDE.md` "Reporting from a card".
