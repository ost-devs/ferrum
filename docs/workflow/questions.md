# Open questions about the method

Questions for the consultant about how the work runs (`docs/workflow/README.md` §7), in the shape of `CLAUDE.md` "Raising a question", ids `M<n>` (the next is M39). The orchestrator adds one when an observation calls the method into question or a stop-loss fires; the consultant removes it when it records the decision (`docs/decisions/method.md`).

## M34. Progress file regressed into append-only history with stale pointers, duplicated counters, and contradictory instructions despite the 2026-11-13 decision
- **Found.** In `docs/decisions/method.md` (2026-11-13, decision 4), the progress file was shrunk *"to where the work is (it had grown to 35 KB loaded into every session)"* when `docs/progress/ledger.jsonl` was introduced. In `ferrum-state.md` (which opens by citing `docs/workflow/README.md` §5: *"Where the work is, and nothing else"*):
  1. **Stale top-level pointers and duplicated counters.**
     - `Stage:` still reports that the implementation stage (`docs/workflow/implementation.md`) is done and cites `dev/maintainer/reports/2026-11-19-ferrum-state-implementacao.md`, without reflecting Conformance Step 5 (b).
     - `Read before acting:` still directs a new session to `docs/workflow/implementation.md` and `docs/parity/ferrum-state/plan.md` "Implementation", rather than Conformance documents.
     - `Decisions in force:` duplicates the next-ID counters of the questions files — it states that `docs/parity/ferrum-state/questions.md` holds Q141 and Q142 (next ID `Q143`) and `docs/workflow/questions.md` holds M29 (next `M30`), whereas `ferrum-state.md` itself records decisions up to `Q150` and `M32`, and `docs/decisions/method.md` already records `M33`.
  2. **Active contradictions left in prose.**
     - The 72 mutation timeouts are described mid-paragraph as running detached with `--timeout 300` and an instruction (*"if the session ends, run it again with `--iterate`"*), while the end of the same paragraph reports that both the 300 s and 3,000 s reruns finished with no timeouts left.
     - `control all` and G5 are described as *"Running in the background"*, and reported finished further down in the same block.
     - The maintainer's 2026-11-19 note still carries the pending item to return the branch-coverage choice to the consultant *"before conformance starts"*, although Conformance is at Step 5 (b).
  3. **Current operational sequence buried inside a closed item.** The entire Conformance stage (Steps 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (a), 5 (b) through C2, G8 timed runs, and timeout reruns) is appended as a single unbroken prose block inside item `3. From `ferrum-state.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/workflow/questions.md`, and `docs/decisions/method.md` alone, it is not settled what boundaries keep a progress file authoritative for a cold-start session, when its fields must be updated, or how cold-start continuity is verified so this drift does not recur.
- **Bears on it.**
  - A fresh session with no conversation memory cannot trust the top-level fields of `ferrum-state.md` without risking concrete operational errors: reading the wrong workflow documents, re-running finished background jobs (`--iterate`), trusting stale counters, or missing active constraints buried in closed narrative.
  - Duplicating state that already has an authoritative home (such as next-ID counters) creates guaranteed drift whenever one copy is updated without the other.
  - Even when the real state can still be reconstructed from the full file, every append increases interpretation cost and raises the risk that a new session repeats a completed step, applies a superseded rule, or misses a later decision.
  - Because `ferrum-state.md` holds real, verifiable progress (commits, check passes, decision references), indiscriminate summarization or deletion would risk losing active constraints or valid checkpoints.
  - Unverified: whether existing orchestrator rules, hooks, or scripts already require checkpoint updates or cold-start verification that were not followed here.
- **Blocks.** Reliable cold-start continuity for the remainder of `ferrum-state` (from Conformance Step 5 (b) `C3` onward), `Improvement delta`, and preventing the same degradation in future crates (starting before `ferrum-view` S0).
- **Raised by.** Maintainer, 2026-11-27.

## M35. Execution history and audit details accumulate inside the progress file without a clear boundary against existing records or a verified need for a separate log
- **Found.** `docs/decisions/method.md` (2026-11-13 decision 4, 2026-11-19, and M23) established `docs/progress/ledger.jsonl` (`tools/ledger.py`) to record runs, tokens, consumed usage, defects, and expectations, and `ferrum-state.md` already links to `docs/decisions/ferrum-state.md`, `docs/decisions/method.md`, `docs/parity/ferrum-state/verification.md`, `docs/parity/ferrum-state/conformance/`, and `dev/maintainer/reports/`. Despite those artifacts, `Done` and item 3 of `Next, in order` in `ferrum-state.md` retain dense execution history for closed stages (R, U1–U4, Conformance Steps 0–4): per-run token totals and budgets, attempt counts, usage-limit cut logs (2026-11-13 to 2026-11-15), intermediate commit hashes, per-round mutant/line/branch tallies, and how each defect was discovered and retried.
- **Open because.** `tools/ledger.py`, `docs/parity/ferrum-state/verification.md`, `docs/parity/ferrum-state/conformance/`, `dev/maintainer/reports/`, and `.claude/skills/ferrum-run/notes.md` were not inspected here. It is not settled:
  - Which categories of execution detail currently in `ferrum-state.md` are already preserved in `tools/ledger.py`, stage reports, or verification files (pure duplication).
  - Which categories of audit history (e.g., intermediate commits, per-checkpoint evidence chains, retry/interruption narratives, evolution of findings across rounds) are not captured anywhere else and would be lost if removed from `ferrum-state.md`.
  - Whether a separate execution or checkpoint log is actually needed, or whether existing artifacts already cover the audit function once the progress file stops duplicating them.
- **Bears on it.**
  - Three distinct kinds of content are currently mixed in `ferrum-state.md`: (1) verifiable current progress and active checkpoints, (2) technical audit history (commits, verification outputs, finding evolution), and (3) repetitive operational narrative (per-run token counts, budget ratios, usage-limit interruptions and resumes).
  - Retaining categories (2) and (3) inside the progress file degrades its function as a resumption instrument, while deleting them without verifying coverage in `ledger.py` and stage reports risks losing auditability.
  - Unverified: the exact schema of `tools/ledger.py` and the contents of `dev/maintainer/reports/` and `verification.md`.
- **Blocks.** Pruning `ferrum-state.md` safely without losing audit evidence, and establishing a durable rule for where execution history lives across all crates.
- **Raised by.** Maintainer, 2026-11-27.

## M37. Append-only accumulation, stale pointers, and superseded entries also appear in living documents outside the progress file — including `questions.md` and the consultant's own `MEMORY.md` — and the rest of the method is unaudited for this drift
- **Found.** Unlike immutable historical records that are intentionally append-only by design (`docs/decisions/` — *"Oldest first; a decision that replaces an earlier one says so, and the earlier entry stays"*), living documents and binding texts are meant to reflect current state and active rules. Inspecting only four files (`ferrum-state.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/workflow/questions.md`, and `MEMORY.md`) alongside `docs/decisions/method.md` already reveals the same drift pattern outside `ferrum-state.md`:
  1. **`docs/workflow/questions.md`:** its header still stated `(the next is M33)` even though `M33` was already decided and recorded in `docs/decisions/method.md` on 2026-11-27 (and cited in `MEMORY.md`), creating a real risk of ID collision.
  2. **Consultant's `MEMORY.md`:**
     - Keeps superseded precedents alongside their replacements: `Worker effort` (`M1/M6 commit`) remains above `Worker effort, revised` (`method commit of 2026-11-13`), although `docs/decisions/method.md` (2026-11-13) explicitly replaced the M1 effort rule.
     - Keeps precedents for a mechanism that was dropped for future crates: `Row tags/criteria` (`0cab8fb`) and `Decision tags beyond D1` (`M7 commit`) remain inline as active precedents for `deps`, even though `docs/decisions/method.md` (2026-11-13, decision 7) dropped `deps` for the crates that follow (while `ferrum-state` itself still carries them).
     - Retains raw commit-hash pointers (`5b074d1`, `b9f1dbe`, `2b6f964`, `2595de0`, `a3b36a9`, `7aae14b`, `be272c7`, `0cab8fb`, `4483420`, `M1/M6 commit`, `Same commit`, `Same entry`) despite its own header specifying *"pointer to its entry in docs/decisions/"* and its own precedent stating *"A record that must be found later is a file, never a commit message"*.
  3. **Asymmetry in maintenance loops:** `CLAUDE.md` ("Reporting from a card") defines an explicit pruning loop for worker cards (`docs/workflow/README.md` §5), whereas no equivalent drift check prevented `ferrum-state.md`, `docs/workflow/questions.md`, or `MEMORY.md` from accumulating stale or superseded entries.
- **Open because.** The remaining living documents across the repository were not inspected — including `docs/workflow/` (`README.md`, `evolution.md`, stage briefs), `.claude/rules/`, `.claude/agents/`, the linked memory files (`precedents-proof.md`, `precedents-conformance.md`, `budgets.md`, `feedback-maintainer-suggestions.md`), `.claude/skills/` (such as `.claude/skills/ferrum-run/notes.md`), `docs/parity/ferrum-state/` (`plan.md`, `cards/`, `verification.md`, `questions.md`), and `tools/README.md`. It is unknown where else across the method living or binding files have drifted out of date, accumulated superseded rules, or duplicated state.
- **Bears on it.**
  - This drift is not caused by progress files specifically; any living document updated iteratively across sessions (binding texts, workflow docs, orchestrator notes, questions headers, plans, rules, cards, or the consultant's own memory) is exposed to stale pointers, duplicated state, and superseded entries when there is no discipline keeping it current.
  - Immutable decision logs (`docs/decisions/`) stay append-only by design so history and reasoning remain auditable; the failure mode is applying append-only habits to living documents that agents load as current truth.
  - Fixing `ferrum-state.md` in isolation leaves the rest of the method — across orchestrator, workers, and consultant — unaudited for the same loss of discipline.
  - Unverified: the actual state of every living and binding file outside the five inspected here, and whether `docs/workflow/evolution.md` or `docs/workflow/README.md` already defines a periodic review for them.
- **Blocks.** Confidence that the method as a whole — all living/binding texts, orchestrator state, and the consultant's own memory — stays current and free of stale or contradictory entries before `Improvement delta` and `ferrum-view` S0.
- **Raised by.** Maintainer, 2026-11-27.

## M38. Pruning completed-crate context and older precedents risks losing re-entry knowledge needed for cm6 version deltas, new features, or crate revisions
- **Found.** Completed crates are not closed permanently under the method:
  - `docs/decisions/method.md` (2026-11-09, decision 10) states that a capability later found missing in a conforming crate goes through that crate's stages again.
  - `docs/decisions/method.md` (2026-11-13, improvement delta) schedules native improvements as deltas after conformance, and `ferrum-state.md` tracks `@codemirror/state` at a pinned release (`6.7.6`) that will later face upstream cm6 version updates.
  - `docs/decisions/method.md` (M22, 2026-11-24) requires every finished crate's checks to run again on a Rust toolchain upgrade.
  - At the same time, `ferrum-state.md` and `MEMORY.md` contain crate-specific or older-method details that are irrelevant while working on a different crate (e.g., `ferrum-view`), yet relevant if `ferrum-state` is reopened: exact `--since` check commits (`handoff --since text=c0ba180,change=ea896a1,rangeset=126dad7,state=efdebd2`), crate-specific harness/environment quirks (such as `@marijn/find-cluster-break` loading `dist/index.cjs`), and older precedents that still apply to `ferrum-state`'s existing rows (such as `deps` rules from M3/M7, which were dropped only for crates that follow) or explain why a rejected approach should not be re-tried.
- **Open because.** `docs/workflow/README.md` and `docs/workflow/evolution.md` were not inspected here. It is not settled how the method separates **dormant re-entry context** from both **active context** (what the current crate needs) and **disposable noise** (expired intermediate state), so that:
  - work on a later crate (such as `ferrum-view`) does not load irrelevant context from finished crates;
  - pruning living files (`ferrum-state.md`, `MEMORY.md`, and other living docs) does not erase what is needed to cleanly reopen a finished crate for a cm6 version delta, a new feature, a revision, or a toolchain upgrade.
- **Bears on it.**
  - Treating pruning as simple deletion creates two opposing failure modes: either re-entry context is lost (forcing expensive reconstruction or repeating solved mistakes when a crate is reopened for a cm6 delta or feature), or agents resist pruning out of fear of losing future context and revert to append-only bloat.
  - The same tension applies to the consultant's `MEMORY.md`: removing older or superseded precedents outright can lose institutional memory about why an approach was rejected or how an older crate's artifacts were built, while keeping them inline beside active rules bloats and confuses current decisions.
  - Unverified: whether `docs/workflow/` already defines a re-entry or version-delta procedure for completed crates, and which files are expected to hold a finished crate's durable re-entry state once its active progress file closes.
- **Blocks.** Designing the pruning rules for `ferrum-state.md` and `MEMORY.md` (M34–M37) without breaking future cm6 version deltas, feature additions, or revisions on completed crates.
- **Raised by.** Maintainer, 2026-11-27.
